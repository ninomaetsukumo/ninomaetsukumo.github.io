# JP-03 — Henoko / Doshisha School-Trip Boat Capsizing

**Project Blackbird public report**  
**Research cut-off:** 21 September 2026  
**Status:** Closed as an open-source research snapshot. Reopen only for material new evidence, including a JTSB final report, prosecutorial/court disposition, newly released official records, or substantial first-hand testimony.

## Executive summary

On 16 March 2026, two small vessels, *Fukutsu* and *Heiwamaru*, capsized off Henoko/Oura Bay during a Doshisha International High School training trip. Eighteen students were aboard with three operator-side adults. No teacher boarded either vessel. Student Tomoka Takeishi, 17, and *Fukutsu* captain Hajime Kanai died. Public reporting states that judicial autopsy found drowning as the cause of death for both deceased persons.

Project Blackbird examined how this activity became part of a recurring school program, what safety controls existed, what warning signs were available before and during the voyage, and what can and cannot be concluded from the public record.

The strongest supported conclusion is institutional, not conspiratorial: the accident followed a chain of deficient due diligence, weak risk governance, unclear marine-operational responsibility, inadequate supervision, and day-of decisions that failed to respond adequately to worsening risk. Doshisha's Special Investigation Committee (SIC) went further and stated that deficiencies in the school's safety-management system were the "greatest cause of the accident."

This does not resolve every question. The SIC was commissioned to examine Doshisha's conduct and did not adjudicate the civil or criminal liability of every individual. Technical capsize causation remains for the Japan Transport Safety Board (JTSB), and individual criminal responsibility remains for the Coast Guard, prosecutors and courts.

The evidence does not support a theory that the U.S. military caused the accident. The Henoko base issue explains the educational and political setting, but the operational decisions documented in the public record were made by Japanese school and boat-side actors.

## The three central questions

### 1. Why were students placed on these boats?

The boat component was not procured through an ordinary passenger-vessel selection process. It grew out of an existing educational relationship with Hajime Kanai, a pastor, long-time Henoko contact and boat captain.

Kanai proposed taking students offshore. Teachers saw educational value and adopted the activity as an independent school program. The travel company did not supply or control the boat excursion, and the school did not replace the travel company's normal controls with an equivalent marine-specific verification process.

The public record does not show a structured comparison of licensed passenger operators, vessel suitability, route planning, weather thresholds, emergency procedures, insurance scope, or staffing standards before the program was adopted.

MLIT later determined that Kanai had repeatedly transported students and teachers in circumstances requiring passenger-transport business registration under the Maritime Transportation Act, but had not obtained that registration. MLIT filed a complaint with the Coast Guard on 22 May 2026.

The defensible explanation is therefore:

> A relationship of trust and educational familiarity substituted for the level of independent marine-operator due diligence that the activity required.

There is no public evidence that the structure was deliberately designed to avoid liability or regulation.

### 2. Why did the boats depart without teachers?

The school had no clear rule requiring a teacher to be aboard.

The SIC reconstructed conflicting teacher accounts, but multiple students said both teachers were together on shore and waved as the boats departed. The committee concluded that both teachers accepted departure without a supervising teacher aboard.

A six-month follow-up report adds an important student perspective: one student later told Tomoka's family that they had assumed the excursion would remain within sight of the teachers. When the boats went farther away and the teachers disappeared from view, the student became anxious and wondered whether the situation was really safe.

The issue was therefore not only the physical absence of a teacher. It was the absence of any school-side onboard safety function once the vessels left shore.

The SIC stated that Doshisha left onboard safety entirely to the captains and crew and performed no onboard student safety management.

### 3. Why did Tomoka die?

The broad causal chain is substantially documented; the precise medical and technical mechanism is not fully public.

The evidence shows:

- a wave advisory was active for Nago;
- the school had no advisory-specific cancellation rule;
- the relevant teachers did not properly check and share advisory information;
- Kanai effectively made the go/no-go decision;
- no teacher boarded;
- the captains had no agreed route;
- the Coast Guard later warned that waves were high;
- Kanai acknowledged that warning;
- the voyage continued;
- students later expressed fear as conditions worsened;
- *Fukutsu* capsized at about 10:10;
- *Heiwamaru* capsized about two minutes later while approaching the first casualty;
- Tomoka was later found inside the overturned *Heiwamaru*.

Public follow-up reporting states that judicial autopsy determined drowning as the cause of death. Fire/rescue material cited by the SIC described Tomoka as being found inside the overturned vessel with her lifejacket ridden up or caught against the vessel structure, but the full forensic report is not public.

The family separately reported that her body had numerous visible and covered injuries. The public record does not establish the type, timing, mechanism, or medical contribution of those injuries.

Accordingly:

> The public record supports drowning as the reported forensic cause of death, within a documented chain of institutional and operational safety failures. It does not publicly resolve the exact sequence by which Tomoka became trapped, the contribution of specific injuries, or every technical factor in the second capsize.

## Program origin and prior warning signs

The Henoko field-study program existed before the boat component. The boat excursion was introduced after Kanai proposed an offshore view of the construction area.

Several earlier events should have triggered formal re-evaluation:

- **2023:** a student reflection described the boat as smaller than expected, expressed anxiety about whether it was suitable for the sea, and described roughness/waves. At least two teachers saw the reflection, but no durable marine-safety review followed.
- **2023–2024:** encounters with Coast Guard/security vessels caused student fear. In 2024, some students received loudspeaker warnings and recorded those experiences in reflections. Teachers discussed them, but they did not become a formal safety escalation.
- **2024:** two boats were expected but only *Fukutsu* appeared. Seventeen students were rotated through the vessel. The reason for the missing second boat was not formally established.
- **2025:** the boat activity was cancelled because of rain/sea conditions, but no written cancellation threshold was created.
- **2025:** Kanai himself told the school audience that the sea was dangerous, that captains had to pay close attention to safety, and that a fellow captain had died during maritime activity.

The important point is not that any one warning predicted the 2026 capsizing. It is that repeated marine-risk signals accumulated without producing a formal revalidation of the activity.

## Day-of risk chain

The strongest day-of reconstruction is a sequence, not a single mistake.

1. A wave advisory was already in force.
2. The school had no agreed response rule for advisory-only conditions.
3. Teachers did not independently verify marine conditions to a defined threshold.
4. Kanai said the sea was calm and made the operational go decision.
5. No teacher boarded.
6. The captains had not agreed a specific route.
7. At about 09:48, a Coast Guard officer repeatedly warned by loudspeaker that waves were high and advised caution.
8. Kanai acknowledged the warning.
9. The voyage continued.
10. Students were allowed to handle steering controls during parts of the excursion.
11. Student accounts later described substantial speed and fear.
12. On the return, students described the sea becoming darker, waves becoming rougher and speed increasing.
13. Students described seeing a large wave approaching and shouting.
14. *Fukutsu* capsized.
15. *Heiwamaru* capsized shortly afterward during the response to the first capsize.

ABC/TV Asahi reporting based on student accounts is especially useful because it predates the SIC and independently records key elements later found in the committee report: fear, increasing speed, student steering, worsening sea conditions and the wave immediately before capsize.

The public evidence supports the statement that Kanai received and acknowledged a high-wave warning and continued the excursion. It does not establish his subjective motive, state of mind, or criminal guilt.

## Student supervision and emergency preparedness

All students were wearing lifejackets, but the SIC found inconsistent instruction and no school-wide marine-emergency briefing. Correct fit for every student could not be verified.

Emergency response also depended heavily on students:

- after the capsizes, multiple students called the Coast Guard themselves;
- an operator-side adult reportedly gave students an incorrect Coast Guard telephone number before successful calls were made;
- survivors experienced violent immersion, repeated waves, disorientation, entrapment risk and panic.

The Coast Guard later interviewed all 17 surviving students, reportedly with guardians present, and obtained student-shot video. Those underlying interview records are not publicly accessible. Project Blackbird tested the 11th Regional Coast Guard and Nakagusuku public web structure and found no open database or indexed page containing the raw statements.

## Tomoka's attempted course change

The SIC established that Tomoka had requested to move out of the Henoko course before the trip. She remained assigned to it after a lottery because the number of students seeking changes exceeded available capacity.

This is important context, but the reason for her request is not established publicly.

It would be improper to claim that she sought a change because she believed the boat activity was unsafe. The available record supports only that she requested a transfer and did not receive one.

## Parent information and informed participation

Parents were not given a detailed marine-risk explanation covering the vessel type, protest use, route, registration status, teacher-boarding arrangements and marine-specific controls.

The SIC found that the school breached its duty to provide sufficient explanation for informed participation.

The family later said they had not understood that Tomoka would board what were commonly used as protest boats. That family perspective is consistent with the broader information asymmetry documented by the SIC, but intent to conceal has not been established.

The political identity of the boats is relevant to transparency and educational governance. It is not, by itself, evidence of why the vessels capsized.

## Family notification failure

The notification chronology is one of the clearest post-accident governance failures.

By about 12:18, school management knew Tomoka had been transported in cardiopulmonary arrest. Death was confirmed at about 12:29.

The family learned that she had been taken to hospital through another student's parent before receiving formal school notification. This account appears consistently in family reporting and the SIC chronology.

The evidence supports a finding that formal school communication lagged informal parent/student networks. It does not establish deliberate concealment.

## School response and reforms

Doshisha's response became more substantial after the accident:

- an external Special Investigation Committee was established;
- the SIC report was published on 31 July;
- the school was searched by the 11th Regional Coast Guard Headquarters in July in connection with suspected professional negligence causing death/injury;
- the Doshisha chair announced an intention to resign after completing accountability and reform work;
- the Doshisha International principal was removed effective 31 August;
- a new Safety Management Office was established on 1 September;
- the office was given authority to create common safety standards, review and approve high-risk activities, monitor safety management, issue corrective instructions and conduct staff training;
- the 2026 Okinawa training trip was cancelled;
- a separate committee on educational neutrality and appropriateness was planned.

These reforms are evidence that the corporation regarded the prior system as requiring fundamental change. They are not, by themselves, admissions of every alleged legal breach.

## Political and educational context

The Henoko excursion existed within a politically contested setting. The vessels and principal operator were connected to anti-base maritime activity.

MEXT separately concluded on 22 May that the educational activity, as conducted, raised an Article 14(2) issue under the Basic Act on Education and required review of political neutrality and appropriateness.

That question must remain separate from accident causation.

The current evidence does not show that students themselves were conducting protest maneuvers at the time of the capsizes, nor does it show that U.S. military personnel or operations caused the accident.

## What the evidence does not establish

Project Blackbird found no reliable public evidence establishing:

- deliberate intent by the school to expose students to danger;
- a conspiracy involving Doshisha, activist groups or U.S. authorities;
- a deliberate attempt to conceal the accident from the family;
- an order by teachers to destroy student videos;
- a criminally negligent individual as a matter of final legal judgment;
- that Tomoka requested a course change because of safety fears;
- that her injuries, rather than drowning, were the medical cause of death;
- a final civil settlement or compensation outcome;
- a final JTSB technical causation report;
- a final prosecutorial disposition as of the research cut-off.

## Accountability map

### Doshisha International High School / School Corporation Doshisha

Strongest public findings concern:

- inadequate prior risk investigation;
- insufficient explanation to students and parents;
- weak school-trip safety governance;
- failure to establish advisory-based go/no-go criteria;
- absence of onboard school supervision;
- failure to independently verify the marine activity;
- weak escalation of prior warning signals.

The SIC expressly described the school's safety-management deficiencies as the greatest cause of the accident.

### Hajime Kanai / operator side

Documented issues include:

- passenger transport without the registration later determined by MLIT to be required;
- operational go/no-go authority concentrated in Kanai;
- no agreed route among captains;
- Coast Guard high-wave warning received and acknowledged;
- continuation after the warning;
- student accounts of increasing speed and fear;
- sole operator-side adult aboard *Fukutsu* despite evidence of a prior two-person operating practice.

These facts may be relevant to negligence analysis, but Project Blackbird does not make a criminal-guilt determination.

### Regulators / investigators

The Coast Guard conducted rescue and criminal investigation activity. JTSB has the separate technical-investigation role. The existence of several processes means institutional, technical and criminal causation should not be collapsed into one conclusion.

## Final assessment

The public evidence is sufficient to answer the parents' first two questions substantially and the third question at a broad causal level.

**Why were students placed on these boats?**  
Because a trusted educational relationship evolved into an independent marine activity without equivalent professional marine-operator due diligence and school-controlled safety verification.

**Why did they depart without teachers?**  
Because the school had no clear teacher-onboard requirement and both supervising teachers accepted departure without onboard school supervision.

**Why did Tomoka die?**  
Public reporting states that judicial autopsy found drowning. The accident occurred after a documented chain of inadequate institutional safety controls, an active wave advisory, no teacher aboard, an acknowledged Coast Guard high-wave warning, continued navigation, worsening conditions and two capsizes. The exact technical mechanism of the second capsize and the precise sequence of Tomoka's entrapment remain unresolved in the public record.

Project Blackbird therefore closes JP-03 as an **open-source research snapshot**, not as a declaration that every official investigation is complete.

The case should be reopened only if material new evidence appears: a JTSB final report, prosecutorial or court disposition, newly released investigative records, substantive new survivor testimony, or new forensic/medical documentation.
