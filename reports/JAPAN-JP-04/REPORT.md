## Executive summary

Japan's current foreign-national policy landscape is better described as **differentiated economic access alongside targeted immigration and administrative control** than as a single uniform policy of restriction. The retained official evidence documents labour recruitment and intake pathways, more demanding entry and compliance rules for certain statuses, differentiated application fees, enforcement directed at specified violations, and legislated pre-travel screening for specified visitors. These instruments address different populations; they cannot fairly be collapsed into a claim about all foreign nationals. [S-001; F-002–F-024]

Across the studied industries, labour-market and recruitment pressures coexist with growing foreign participation. Government models specify prospective foreign intake needs after assumed domestic labour and productivity measures. They are **conditional planning models**, not an observed number of indispensable foreign workers. National structural dependence, total production loss without these workers, and exact calibration of each intake ceiling remain untested on the available evidence. [S-002; F-001/F-017–F-022]

Specific official rationales are well documented: immigration compliance, the PR/naturalisation policy-consistency objective, worker protection and managed labour pathways, administrative finance, and earlier screening of relevant travellers. Such a **DIRECT TRIGGER** is evidence that the government linked a problem to a measure, **not proof that the response works, is proportionate, or was caused by timing alone**. Broad claims about collective abuse, fee deterrence, aggregate fiscal damage, and future JESTA benefits lack corresponding causal proof. [S-003/S-004/S-007; F-003–F-008/F-013–F-024]

## 1. Research question and methodology

**Question:** Do Japan's foreign-resident and visitor policy measures match the government's own statistics on the problems they purport to address and the roles foreign workers and visitors play?

This is a bounded documentary assessment of official legislation, guidance, public data and recorded arguments as assembled in the underlying modules. It is **not** an estimate of immigration's net contribution to Japan's GDP, an assessment of every nationality or individual, an accusation of institutional misconduct, or a causal evaluation of newly implemented systems.

Every major conclusion follows **SOURCE → FACT → ANALYSIS → LIMITATION**. Classification terminology:
**CONFIRMED** — supported by identified documentary record; **COUNTED** — reported observed count; **ATTRIBUTED** — explicitly assigned to an identified speaker/authority; **DERIVED** — reproducible consequence of stated inputs; **INFERRED** — analyst interpretation; **NOT FOUND** — not established in the completed research; **NOT PUBLISHED** — affirmative evidence of nonpublication, not merely a failed download; **UNTESTABLE** — cannot be validly resolved from current data/design; **DISPUTED** — source-level conflict requiring disclosure.

Trigger classifications are **DIRECT TRIGGER**, **POTENTIAL TRIGGER POINT**, **POLITICAL USE** and **NO ESTABLISHED LINK**. An explicit rationale must not be equated with causation, effectiveness or the economic optimality of a number. Citations such as [F-021] link to the case's research finding, which links to underlying evidence IDs and primary-source locators; this draft requires a further claim-by-claim source audit before publication.

## 2. Economic evidence: five channels

### 2.1 Labour supply and productive/service capacity

Food service shows labour-recruitment pressure and a growing foreign workforce, but raw vacancy ratios, wages, turnover and counts do not establish nationwide inability to operate without foreign workers. Care, agriculture and manufacturing have distinct demographic, recruitment and labour-supply profiles; an aggregate description would obscure those distinctions. [F-001; F-019/F-021]

Official FY2028 residual intake planning in the completed research records **160,700 in Care, 99,600 in Agriculture, and 319,200 in industrial-product Manufacturing** after modelled domestic-productivity/recruitment responses. These quantities are **ATTRIBUTED forecasts**, not actual empty positions, national dependency percentages or unavoidable realised intake. The agriculture model decomposition was reconciled in the frozen research and should not be replaced by an earlier inconsistent conversational figure. [F-018/F-020/F-022]

**Assessment (DERIVED):** Selective foreign-labour recruitment has documented economic planning rationales. **Limit (UNTESTABLE):** Exact intake calibration, nationally representative substitution costs, foregone output, matched hours/FTE and national structural indispensability.

### 2.2 Business entry, operations and investment exposure

The revised Business Manager requirements and fee structures affect the legal/administrative conditions for specified prospective applicants. A capital threshold is not identical to operating expenditure or a cash reserve that must remain untouched. Comparisons with foreign regimes require population, wage, rent, purchasing-power and legal-route normalization; the research did not establish a published normalized derivation for Japan's ¥30 million threshold. [F-002/F-023]

Restaurant insolvencies increased in periods preceding the BM reform. That fact cannot establish that the later reform caused these failures. Restaurants remain in principle eligible under the BM framework, and an existing PR holder is not automatically subjected to Business Manager requalification. [F-011/F-012]

**Assessment (DERIVED):** Entry feasibility and financial exposure can change for affected prospective entrepreneurs. **Limit:** No representative post-reform restaurant closure cohort, matched investment counterfactual, or general economic-loss estimate.

### 2.3 Long-term settlement, compliance and household costs

PR admission, post-grant PR cancellation, and naturalisation are legally distinct decisions with different requirements and effects. The record supports a specific consistency rationale connecting PR and naturalisation changes; it does not demonstrate that naturalisation as a whole became easier or that a single quantitative cutoff was calibrated to individual applicant risk. Language/integration scrutiny has historical antecedents. [F-003–F-006/F-013/F-014]

Official pension and insurance counts document particular nonpayment populations. They do not provide a representative numerator for **intentional PR-specific nonpayment**, and a foreign share of a nonpayment pool is not proof of a higher nationality-specific propensity or intentional abuse. Differentiated application charges and reductions are documented, but annual household costs depend on procedure, duration, channel and eligibility. [F-007/F-008/F-023]

**Assessment (DERIVED):** Observable compliance requirements and potential costs differ by route. **Limit:** A household burden rate, aggregate abuse rate, causal departure effect and individual intent remain **NOT FOUND/UNTESTABLE**.

### 2.4 Visitors, demand and advance screening

Visitor arrivals and spending show a substantial demand channel, while reported overstay numbers describe separate status and time populations. Temporary Visitor as a prior status is not equivalent to every ordinary leisure tourist. Neither the overstay stock nor landing refusal cases may be divided by an annual arrival flow to claim a comparable risk rate. [F-009/F-010]

JESTA, enacted in Act No. 32 of 2026, is designed for principally visa-exempt short-stay travellers and specified additional landing/transit categories. The law's target is introduction in FY2028, with statutory commencement no later than 31 March 2029, but as at the research cutoff the system was **not operational** and the final fee and exact launch date were **NOT FOUND**. Ordinary residents returning with valid re-entry/deemed re-entry permission are generally outside the ordinary personal authorisation requirement; carrier information obligations should not be conflated with a universal resident permission. [F-024]

**Assessment (DERIVED):** Visitor demand and narrowly targeted pre-entry screening need not be contradictory. **Limit:** Queue effects, tourist deterrence, false positives, prevention, post-entry removal savings and net visitor-economy impact are **UNTESTABLE** before relevant operation/data.

### 2.5 Administrative capacity, enforcement and public finance

Enforcement statistics show differentiated caseloads and mixed directions, not a single measured deterioration across all foreign residents. The precise population addressed by a rule matters more than a broad national headline count. [F-015/F-016]

Fee legislation and implementing tariffs articulate processing, inflation, digital efficiency and broader immigration administration/support funding considerations. The documented October fee schedule is differentiated by procedure, duration and channel, with PR permission charged separately and some reductions/exemptions. Statutory ceilings must not be reported as payable tariffs. Naturalisation has no government application fee in the completed official record. Earlier government fee-revenue examples were hypothetical, not collected receipts or proven deterrence. [F-023]

**Assessment (CONFIRMED documentary rationale; DERIVED interaction):** Financial/administrative purposes are explicit. **Limit:** No demonstrated precise cost-based tariff derivation, annual income-relative distributional impact, deterrence effect, or causal cost-benefit success.

## 3. Cross-policy integration

| Policy interaction | What the evidence establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Labour access × control | Managed new pathways coexist with tighter checks, protections, numerical/regional conditions [F-017–F-022] | Aggregate net opening/closing or optimal intake |
| Business entry × compliance cost | Different requirements apply to some prospective managers and status applicants [F-002/F-012/F-023] | General business-collapse effect |
| PR × naturalisation | Express consistency rationale; separate legal decisions [F-003–F-006/F-013/F-014] | Relative ease or universal impact on existing PR |
| Tourism × JESTA | Large visitor demand and targeted screening rationale coexist [F-009/F-010/F-024] | Post-launch screening success or damage |
| Public burden allegations × measured populations | Pension, insurance, PR and enforcement cohorts differ [F-003/F-007/F-008/F-015/F-016] | Group-wide intentional misconduct |
| Fees × enforcement finance | Express administration/support and control-finance rationale [F-023] | Proven deterrence or welfare burden justification |

The integrated evidence therefore supports **differentiation of policy instruments**, not a claim that all foreigners are treated alike. Equally, this observation does not validate every enacted requirement or discount genuine individual compliance violations. [S-001/S-003/S-007]

## 5. Conclusions

**S-001 — Differentiated policy instruments.** Japan's recorded 2026 measures combine selective economic intake with specific compliance, financing and screening requirements; coverage differs by legal category. A uniform foreigner-wide narrative is not supported by this instrument-level analysis.

**S-002 — Real sector pressures; conditional intake estimates.** Sector-specific evidence and government forecasts support the existence of labour-demand rationales, but national indispensability and exact thresholds cannot be inferred.

**S-003 — Do not generalise adverse categories.** Discrete enforcement or payment counts are not population-wide intentional-abuse measures; denominator and cohort discipline is mandatory.

**S-004 — Rationale is not effectiveness.** Several measures have well-documented official reasons, whereas their effects and proportionality are not established by the reasons themselves.

**S-005 — Cost exposure is heterogeneous.** Rules and fees create differing exposure for business entrants, families and applicants; neither aggregate deterrence nor economy-wide loss has been demonstrated.

**S-006 — Screening and visitor economics are distinct tests.** JESTA's enacted targeted design can coexist with visitor-economy objectives; no postlaunch causal verdict is yet possible.

**S-007 — Evidence-calibration test.** The public-interest question is whether quantitative evidence used to justify a measure matches the legally affected population and chosen threshold. The present record documents some strong links and some unresolved calibration gaps; it does not justify unsupported allegations about motive or aggregate fiscal outcomes.

## 6. Limits, contrary context, and future tests

1. **Stocks vs flows:** PR stock, resident stock, overstay stock, annual arrivals and administrative event counts must remain separate; no cross-population percentage is constructed.
2. **Selected-sector extrapolation:** Care, Agriculture, Manufacturing and Food Service findings do not establish whole-economy causality or essential foreign labour.
3. **Reform timing:** Several compliance and entry designs are too recent, or not yet operational, for a postimplementation effectiveness conclusion. Earlier trends do not demonstrate later-policy impacts.
4. **Source availability:** Some official MOJ/ISA originals could not be directly retrieved; recorded official indexes, legislative records and alternative primary texts were labelled accordingly in the source appendices. An inaccessible URL is not a finding of nonpublication.
5. **Political arguments:** Official or party statements are attributed where recorded. The report neither adopts collective blame nor infers unproven personal motive.
6. **Future updates:** Reopen only the affected F/S conclusion if a material corrected legal text, credible contradictory data, implementation detail or rigorous outcome study becomes available.

### F-003 — PR arrears and denominator / 永住と未納資料

**Primary-source locator (documentary starting point):** [Original-source locator](https://www.shugiin.go.jp/internet/itdb_kaigiroku.nsf/html/kaigiroku/000421320240508017.htm) · [Finding-specific source audit and limits](CLAIM_SOURCE_MATRIX.md)

### F-004 — PR admission versus revocation / 永住許可と取消し

**Primary-source locator (documentary starting point):** [Original-source locator](https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/api/2/law_data/326CO0000000319_20270401_506AC0000000060) · [Finding-specific source audit and limits](CLAIM_SOURCE_MATRIX.md)

**Documented facts and measures.** new PR-specific statutory grounds/status-change provisions take effect 2027-04-01. The statute provides notice, opinion hearing and evidence rights and requires another status except where continued residence is considered inappropriate. Recovered guidance distinguishes deliberate nonpayment from hardship, repayment arrangements and employer-remittance failures; some post-PR conduct before implementation can be considered. Approval guidance and BM-to-PR eligibility concern permission of a new application; BM renewal transition is separate.

**Assessment.** an admission negative factor is not an automatic PR-holder cancellation ground; applicability of a cancellation ground and choice of cancellation/status change are distinct stages. No blanket “late payment causes deportation” or “all existing PR holders must meet the new admission tests” finding is supported.

**Evidence boundary.** e-Gov confirms adoption and no opinion-driven modification, not byte identity of the drafts and final MOJ pages. Written safeguards do not measure future enforcement. Specific alternative status/term and individual outcomes are not guaranteed by these summaries.

**Source trace.** current/future e-Gov Immigration Act versions PR-L1/L2; recovered 2026 approval/revocation drafts and replies PR-A1/A2/R1/R2; adoption metadata PR-A0/R0; retained checked ISA BM document PR-BM1. See the [public primary-source locator index](SOURCES.md).

### F-007 — National Pension comparison / 国民年金

**Primary-source locator (documentary starting point):** [Original-source locator](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/topics/bukyoku/nenkin/nenkin/toukei/dl/k_r06.pdf) · [Finding-specific source audit and limits](CLAIM_SOURCE_MATRIX.md)

**Documented facts and measures.** MHLW reports 770,000 foreign Category 1 insured and 80,000 foreign 24-month non-payers at FY2024 end. Across all Category 1 insured, there were 13.68 million insured and 720,000 24-month non-payers.

**Assessment.** subtracting the foreign count from the national total leaves about **640,000 non-foreign 24-month non-payers**. Using the total 720,000 non-payers as the common denominator, foreign nationals account for **11.1%** and the non-foreign remainder for **88.9%** of the documented 24-month non-payer population.

**POTENTIAL TRIGGER POINT / POPULATION CONTEXT:** this composition is relevant when testing whether the population demonstrated by the evidence corresponds to the population singled out by later policy. It does **not** establish causation and it does not show which group has the higher within-group nonpayment rate.

**Evidence boundary.** 24-month nonpayment is not deliberate nonpayment; foreign-wide pension data are not PR-holder data; the MHLW publication does not give nationality-by-nationality rates; and composition of the non-payer pool is different from propensity to become a non-payer. The selected 2023 PR-supporter arrears sample remains a separate population.

### F-009 — Tourism demand and spending / 観光需要と消費

**Primary-source locator (documentary starting point):** [Original-source locator](https://www.jnto.go.jp/news/_files/20260916_1615.pdf) · [Finding-specific source audit and limits](CLAIM_SOURCE_MATRIX.md)

**Documented facts and measures.** Jan–Aug 訪日外客数 was **24,008,405 (2024), 28,384,099 (2025), 27,626,300 (2026)**. Q2 inbound spending, all second-preliminary releases, was **¥2.1402tn, ¥2.5043tn, ¥2.5125tn**, respectively. Official spending per general visitor was **¥239,028, ¥236,716, ¥244,733**.

**Assessment.** Arrivals rose **18.23%** in 2025, then fell **2.67%** in 2026, remaining **15.07% above 2024**. Q2 nominal spending rose **17.01%**, then **0.33%**, leaving it **17.40% above 2024**. General-visitor average spending rose **3.39%** in 2026. Demand remains economically substantial, with softer arrival volume and nearly flat total spending in the latest comparable period.

**Evidence boundary.** 訪日外客数 is broader than leisure tourists and counts entries, not unique people. JTA excludes Japan-resident foreigners and its general-visitor average excludes cruises. 2026 arrivals remain provisional/estimated; cruise spending has a sampling caveat.
